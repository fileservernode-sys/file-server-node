Product Overview

What is ZdexCloud?

A software platform that turns any spare Android smartphone into a private, self-hosted file server accessible from anywhere.

For Everyday Users

Do you have an old Android smartphone sitting in a desk drawer? ZdexCloud turns that dormant device into your own personal mini-cloud. Instead of paying monthly fees to storage providers who scan your data, you keep your photos, documents, videos, and archives on your phone at home and access them seamlessly from any web browser at work, school, or on the road.

  • No technical networking knowledge required
  • Keep 100% ownership and privacy of your family media and documents
  • Clean, familiar web interface that works on desktop, tablet, and mobile

For Technical Users & Self-Hosters

ZdexCloud is an ultra-low-power, lightweight self-hosted storage host engine. It runs as an Android foreground service, exposes local filesystem trees (internal UFS/eMMC and expandable micro-SD storage), and initiates an authenticated outbound TLS WebSocket connection to a high-throughput streaming relay.

  • Bypasses Carrier-Grade NAT (CGNAT) without static public IP or port forwarding
  • Decoupled authentication: Web Account session vs. independent Server credentials
  • Consumes only 2W–5W typical power with integrated lithium battery fallback

Key Definition (AEO): ZdexCloud is a personal file server system that turns an Android smartphone into a self-hosted server accessible over the internet via an outbound encrypted tunnel, eliminating router port forwarding while preserving physical data locality.

Hardware Repurposing

Why an Old Phone Is the Ideal Micro-Server

Modern smartphones have quad-core ARM chips, gigabytes of RAM, solid-state flash storage, Wi-Fi radios, and built-in battery backup.

Built-in Battery UPS

Unlike a desktop PC or Raspberry Pi that crashes instantly during power brownouts, a smartphone has an onboard battery that prevents sudden filesystem corruption.

2W–5W Ultra-Low Power

Traditional desktop servers draw 60W–150W 24/7. An idle Android device draws 2W–5W, resulting in pennies per month in electricity costs.

Expandable SD Storage

Many Android phones support micro-SD cards up to 512GB or 1TB, giving you extensive offline capacity without monthly subscription fees.

Zero Hardware Purchase

Repurpose equipment you already own instead of buying dedicated NAS hardware or Raspberry Pi boards that cost upwards of $100+.

Important Operating Caveats for Repurposed Phones

To run continuously 24/7, keep your phone connected to its standard AC charger on a stable Wi-Fi network. Ensure aggressive OEM battery optimization is disabled for the ZdexCloud app, and place the device in a well-ventilated area away from direct sunlight to preserve battery health.

System Architecture

How the Architecture Works

A secure three-node topology connecting your web browser to your Android phone via encrypted relay streaming.

1 Client Node

Web Browser Interface

You sign in to zdexcloud.com from any desktop or mobile browser. When you select an active server and provide its access credentials, the browser connects to the encrypted relay gateway.

Standard HTTPS / WSS
2 Stateless Bridge

Encrypted Relay Gateway

Located in our secure cloud infrastructure, the relay gateway verifies session validity and bridges duplex message streams between your browser and Android phone in RAM. No file contents are written to disk on the relay.

Zero Disk Storage Buffer
3 Host Node

Android Phone Server

The phone maintains a persistent outbound WebSocket tunnel to the gateway. When requests arrive, the background engine reads local files from flash storage and streams binary chunks back through the tunnel.

Physical Storage Locality

Step-by-Step Request Lifecycle

  1. Outbound Registration: When started, the Android app connects out to the gateway and registers its unique installationId.
  2. Browser Authentication: You log in to your account with Email + 2FA OTP and unlock your server using your dedicated server password.
  3. Command Tunneling: When you open a folder or stream a video, directory read and chunk requests are tunneled through the relay to your phone.
  4. Local Flash Delivery: The phone reads chunks directly from internal storage or micro-SD and streams them back in real time to your browser player.
Zero Network Hassle

Why No Router Port Forwarding Is Required

Traditional servers require complex router configuration and expose your home network. ZdexCloud eliminates both.

The Old Inbound Approach

Traditional home servers require configuring inbound port forwarding (e.g. port 80/443/22) on your home router and obtaining a public IPv4 address or dynamic DNS.

  • Blocked by CGNAT: Most cellular mobile networks and fiber ISPs use Carrier-Grade NAT, making inbound port forwarding technically impossible.
  • Security Vulnerability: An open inbound port allows automated port scanners and botnets on the public internet to probe your home hardware directly.
  • Complex Configuration: Requires logging into router admin portals, setting static DHCP leases, and handling dynamic IP shifts.

The ZdexCloud Outbound Approach

ZdexCloud uses outbound-only TLS 1.3 connections. Your phone initiates the connection out to the relay gateway just like a standard web browser loads a website.

  • Zero Router Configuration: Works immediately on any home Wi-Fi or cellular hotspot without touching router settings.
  • Immune to CGNAT: Works seamlessly behind carrier-grade NAT, multi-tier residential routers, and mobile tethering networks.
  • Zero Open Ports: No inbound listener is exposed to the public internet, protecting your home LAN from external scans.

Direct Answer (AEO): How does ZdexCloud bypass router port forwarding? Your Android smartphone initiates an outbound WebSocket connection over TLS 1.3 to ZdexCloud's relay. Because all firewalls and NAT routers permit outbound client traffic, bidirectional communication is achieved without opening inbound firewall ports.

Engineering Rigor

Security & Privacy Transparency

We believe in honest, verifiable security claims. Here is how your data is protected — and what our technical boundaries are.

TLS 1.3 In-Flight Encryption

All communications between your browser and the relay, and between the relay and your Android phone, are encrypted over modern TLS 1.3 protocol suites.

Dual-Layer Authentication

Access is protected by two distinct layers: your account session (Email + 2FA OTP) and your local server password. An account compromise alone cannot unlock an encrypted server without the server password.

No Inbound Attack Surface

Because the phone connects out over WebSockets, no listening ports are bound on your local router. Automated internet port scanners will never discover your phone server.

Our Honest Security Boundaries

We avoid unsubstantiated marketing hype. Instead, here are our verified engineering specifications:

  • Relay Privacy: Data packets transit our relay servers in volatile memory buffers to reach your browser. The relay does not write files to persistent disk.
  • Device Security: Because your files are stored locally on your phone, physical security of the device is your responsibility. Keep your host phone protected with an OS lockscreen PIN.
  • Network Security: Always connect your host phone to a trusted home Wi-Fi network with WPA2/WPA3 security enabled.
Hardware Setup Guide

Real-World Reliability: Continuous Server Operation on Android

How to configure your Android phone for continuous, unattended operation without sleep interruptions.

1. Constant AC Power

Connect the phone to a quality 5V/1A or 5V/2A wall charger. Avoid fast wireless charging pads to minimize thermal generation during continuous operation.

2. Stable Wi-Fi Network

Connect the phone to your 2.4GHz or 5GHz home Wi-Fi network. In phone Wi-Fi settings, configure "Keep Wi-Fi on during sleep" to Always.

3. Disable Battery Saver

In Android Settings > Apps > ZdexCloud > Battery, select Unrestricted / Do Not Optimize so Android Doze doesn't pause the background socket.

4. Ambient Ventilation

Remove heavy phone cases and position the phone in a cool, shaded area with natural airflow. This prevents thermal throttling and protects battery longevity.

Architectural Contrast

Is This Cloud Storage?

Understanding the critical difference between commercial centralized storage and self-hosted personal cloud.

No — It's Better: It's Your Personal Server

When people say "cloud storage", they usually mean paying a tech giant to store their files in giant server farms. ZdexCloud provides the convenience of cloud access with the sovereignty of local hardware:

  • You own the storage medium: You decide whether your phone has 64GB or a 1TB micro-SD card. No tiered storage plans.
  • No third-party data profiling: Your pictures, videos, and spreadsheets are not analyzed to build advertising personas.
  • Offline Availability: If the internet goes down, your files are physically right there on your phone at home.

When You Might Still Want Cloud Backups

We practice honest engineering. While ZdexCloud gives you complete day-to-day access and privacy, we recommend following the 3-2-1 backup rule:

  • Hardware Failure: Phones, like all hard drives and flash cards, can eventually suffer hardware failure or battery depletion.
  • Offsite Redundancy: For irreplaceable family photos or critical company accounting books, always maintain an offsite or cold-storage backup.
  • Use in Tandem: Use ZdexCloud as your active daily personal server, and pair it with periodic cold backups for disaster recovery.
