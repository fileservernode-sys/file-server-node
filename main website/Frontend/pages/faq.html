1. General Architecture & Overview

ZdexCloud is a personal file server platform that transforms an unused Android smartphone into a private, remotely accessible file server. Instead of paying perpetual monthly rental fees to corporate cloud providers or discarding functional electronics as electronic waste, ZdexCloud enables you to repurpose mobile hardware into a dedicated personal storage node with global browser-based access.

In commercial cloud services, your files are uploaded to multi-tenant corporate data centers where you pay recurring monthly fees per gigabyte. With ZdexCloud, your files reside directly on your Android phone hardware. ZdexCloud provides the discovery control plane and outbound encrypted WebSocket tunnel, eliminating monthly storage rental fees while keeping physical data custody entirely in your hands.

Traditional self-hosting typically requires dedicated desktop or NAS hardware, higher continuous power draw, complex router port forwarding, static public IP addresses or Dynamic DNS configuration, and manual SSL certificate renewals. ZdexCloud utilizes low-power Android smartphone hardware and an automated outbound TLS 1.3 WebSocket tunnel, providing remote access with zero router network configuration.

Almost any Android smartphone running Android 8.0 (API level 26) or newer with at least 2GB of RAM is fully supported. Older spare phones with cracked screens or missing SIM cards function perfectly as long as the touchscreen allows initial setup and the phone connects to Wi-Fi.

2. Storage & File Hosting Mechanics

Your files remain stored directly on your Android smartphone's internal NAND flash storage or inserted microSD card. Files are not permanently stored, replicated, or staged on ZdexCloud central servers.

Yes. The ZdexCloud Android application allows you to configure storage roots from internal shared storage or mounted removable media such as high-capacity microSD cards (128GB, 256GB, 512GB, 1TB) or supported USB OTG storage.

There is no platform software cap imposed by ZdexCloud. Your total storage capacity is determined strictly by your physical Android device hardware and mounted storage cards. If your phone supports a 1TB microSD card, you have 1TB of private cloud storage without paying extra fees.

3. Remote Access & Gateway Tunneling

No. ZdexCloud operates without router port forwarding, UPnP, or static public IPs. The Android app initiates an outbound TLS 1.3 WebSocket connection to our cloud gateway, which standard home firewalls and mobile carriers permit without opening inbound ports.

Because the connection originates outbound from the Android client to the ZdexCloud connection service over standard HTTPS/WSS port 443, stateful firewall tracking tables keep the bidirectional tunnel open. This bypasses CGNAT barriers common on residential fiber, 4G, and 5G cellular networks.

IP changes do not disrupt your routing identity. When your phone's Wi-Fi router reassigns a local DHCP address or your ISP changes your public WAN IP, the ZdexCloud Android background service automatically detects the network interface transition and re-establishes the outbound tunnel using its persistent cryptographic device token.

Yes. You can log into your ZdexCloud dashboard from any desktop, laptop, tablet, or smartphone web browser anywhere in the world. As long as your Android server phone is powered and connected to the Internet at home, you have full remote access.

4. Android Hardware & App Requirements

ZdexCloud supports Android 8.0 (Oreo / API level 26) through modern Android releases. This broad support ensures that older smartphones manufactured over the past eight years can be successfully repurposed rather than thrown away.

Modern Android operating systems feature aggressive power-saving subsystems (Doze mode, App Standby Buckets, and OEM task killers) that terminate background processes when the screen turns off. Granting Foreground Service permissions and exempting ZdexCloud from battery optimization helps maintain continuous background server operation, subject to device power management and charging conditions.

To protect battery longevity on a dedicated server phone, we recommend: 1) Placing the phone on a flat, well-ventilated surface away from direct sunlight; 2) Using a standard low-power phone charger rather than ultra-fast quick chargers; and 3) Enabling Android's built-in battery protection feature (such as 80% or 85% charge limit) if supported by your device firmware.

No. The phone screen can be completely powered off. The ZdexCloud file engine operates within an Android Foreground Service holding a partial wake lock and Wi-Fi lock, allowing headless continuous server operation while consuming minimal energy.

7. Security & Privacy Architecture

No. ZdexCloud connection gateways act strictly as transit connection layers forwarding data frames between your browser and your phone; personal files are not permanently stored on central disks or scanned for advertising profiling.

All communication between your browser, the ZdexCloud gateway, and the Android phone is secured using standard TLS 1.3 cryptographic transport. This prevents eavesdropping, tampering, and man-in-the-middle attacks across public Wi-Fi networks and transit providers.

ZdexCloud employs a defense-in-depth security model: Platform account credentials authenticate your web session with the control plane, while separate file-server credentials authenticate file access directly on the phone. Even if an account credential were compromised, file-level access requires dedicated authentication against the phone itself.

No. ZdexCloud does not possess administrative bypasses or hidden backdoor credentials into your phone's file storage. The Android application executes strictly within standard sandboxed Android OS permissions and only accesses folders explicitly selected during configuration.

8. Authentication & Strict 24-Hour Session Policy

ZdexCloud enforces a strict 24-hour absolute session lifetime for browser dashboard logins. Exactly 24 hours after signing in, the authentication token expires automatically, requiring you to re-authenticate with your email and OTP code.

Indefinite web sessions pose a critical security vulnerability: if a laptop is lost, stolen, or accessed via an untrusted browser, an open session provides perpetual access to private storage nodes. The 24-hour boundary enforces strict credential rotation and session expiration, mitigating persistent token theft and session hijacking.

No. The Android phone server operates independently of browser login sessions. The phone's background engine and outbound connection continue running continuously in the background. When your browser session expires after 24 hours, only your web browser session ends; your phone server remains online and accessible when you log back in.

Account sign-in requires dual verification: your registered email and password followed by a single-use numeric One-Time Password (OTP) dispatched to your registered email address. This prevents unauthorized account takeovers even if an attacker acquires your password.

10. Reliability, Network Drops & Availability

If the phone loses network connectivity, the web dashboard displays the server as temporarily offline. The ZdexCloud Android background engine continuously monitors network state; the moment Wi-Fi or cellular connectivity returns, the outbound WebSocket tunnel automatically reconnects within seconds.

Yes, provided the 'Start on Boot' toggle is enabled within the ZdexCloud Android app settings. When the phone powers on or completes a software update reboot, Android's boot completed broadcast launches the foreground service and restores server availability automatically.

Unlike traditional PC or NAS servers that instantly crash during power cuts, an Android smartphone contains an integrated battery that functions as an automatic Uninterruptible Power Supply (UPS). If your home router is also on a small UPS or the phone has active cellular data fallback, the server remains online throughout the outage.

11. Data Protection & 3-2-1 Backup Strategy

No. ZdexCloud is a private personal file server and remote access platform, not an automated RAID or offsite disaster recovery service. Storing your files on a single phone means that physical damage, theft, or storage media failure could cause data loss without independent backups.

The industry-standard 3-2-1 backup rule states: Maintain 3 total copies of critical data across 2 different storage media types, with 1 copy located offsite. In a ZdexCloud setup, your Android phone serves as Copy 1; you should periodically duplicate important files to an external hard drive (Copy 2) and retain an encrypted offsite copy or second phone server (Copy 3).

Because ZdexCloud maintains zero copies of your personal data on relay infrastructure, data recovery is impossible from our servers if your physical device fails. We strongly advise backing up irreplaceable photos and documents to an external computer or secondary media card regularly.

12. Troubleshooting & Common Operational Issues

An 'Offline' status typically indicates: 1) The Android phone lost Wi-Fi or cellular Internet connectivity; 2) Server Mode was stopped in the app; 3) The phone ran out of battery; or 4) Android OS power management terminated the background service. Check that the phone has an active network connection, verify the persistent notification is visible in the Android status bar, and ensure battery optimization is disabled.

Aggressive OEM battery managers (such as Xiaomi MIUI/HyperOS, Samsung One UI, Huawei EMUI, or OnePlus OxygenOS) aggressively kill background tasks. To fix this: navigate to Android Settings > Apps > ZdexCloud > Battery > select 'Unrestricted' / 'Don't Optimize', and enable 'Autostart' or 'Background Activity' in your phone's device manager.

File transfer speed is primarily governed by the upload speed of the phone's home Wi-Fi network and the physical read/write speed of the phone's internal flash or microSD card. For optimal speed, connect your phone to a 5GHz Wi-Fi network and use a high-speed UHS-I U3 or V30 rated microSD card.

If camera scanning fails, ensure the browser screen brightness is adequate and clean the phone camera lens. Alternatively, you can bypass the camera scan by signing in directly with your email and password within the Android application.
